## The starting point

**Kako se pravi** ("How it's made") is a site with home recipes and DIY guides: bread, pies, kajmak, rakija, soap and charcoal, explained step by step. It ran on a **PHP magazine script with a database**: every visit had the server assemble the page from PHP and SQL, with a heavy Bootstrap theme on top. Pages were slow, the URLs depended on the script, and every update of the script was a risk.

The client wanted **the same site and the same addresses, but as fast as a site can be**, still on the same ordinary cPanel hosting. The client also asked to keep AdSense, Analytics and both Meta Pixels running, and to have the site redesigned to look like a proper food magazine.

## From PHP to Astro

Instead of copying text by hand, I wrote a **migration script** that read the old site's sitemap and turned every article into a Markdown file, with its cover, the images inside the text, tags, category and publish date.

- **49 articles, 42 tags and the legal pages** moved over automatically.
- **Ten articles lead with a YouTube video.** The script kept the video and its thumbnail as the cover.
- **Headings fixed in every article.** The old editor started at the fourth level and skipped levels; now every article runs in order under a single title.
- The site is built with **Astro 7** and ships as **plain static HTML**: no PHP, no database, nothing the server has to assemble while the reader waits.

## Not a single address lost

For a site that lives on Google traffic, the URLs are the most valuable thing it has. **Every article kept exactly the address it had**, without ".html" and without a trailing slash. A short `.htaccess` serves the static pages on Apache under those addresses, sends the old script's pages (gallery, profile, old RSS feeds) to their nearest new page, and forces https. I tested the rules on Apache before the site went live.

## Speed that holds up with ads

Ads and analytics usually ruin performance, and this site runs **Google AdSense, Google Analytics and two Meta Pixels**: about 650 KB of third-party code.

- These scripts now **load on the first scroll or tap**, or a few seconds after the page opens. Their queues start immediately, so not a single page view is lost, but they no longer stand between the reader and the recipe.
- **Images are converted to WebP** and served in the size each screen needs.
- **YouTube loads only on click.** Until then a video is just one image.
- **Search runs without a server**: the index is built together with the site, so it works on ordinary cPanel hosting.

## SEO

- **Recipe rich results**: recipes with an ingredients list and a method get structured data with every ingredient and every step, so Google can show them as recipes. Nothing is guessed for articles that don't have that structure.
- **Video, breadcrumb and article** markup on every page, a **sitemap with images** for Google Images and `llms.txt` for AI search.
- **Imported articles are credited.** Articles the old script had pulled in from other recipe sites now name their source and point Google to the original, so copied text doesn't pull down the site's own articles.

## A new look

The redesign is a **magazine**: the most-read guide on a large photo, a featured list, categories, a most-read ranking, and on articles a table of contents, a jump-to-recipe button and related articles. At the client's request the site also carries **a banner and a popup after 40 seconds of reading** promoting web design services, with every link tagged so each placement can be measured.
