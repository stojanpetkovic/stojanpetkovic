## The brief

**CARETRANS** is a medical transport company from Belgrade, available **24/7, every day of the year**. It transports bedridden and less mobile patients across Serbia, to the country's spas and brings patients home from Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Italy.

The old site ran on **WordPress** and had **only a few plain pages**: no page per service, no content to rank for, and a slow load on the phones most people use when they urgently need transport. When someone needs an ambulance, they call **the first company whose site opens and tells them what they need to know**. The goal: a faster site that ranks for every service and location the company covers, and turns visits into calls.

## From WordPress to Astro

The site was rebuilt in **Astro and Tailwind CSS** and ships as static HTML: no database, no plugins, nothing that has to be built on the server while the visitor waits. Pages open almost instantly, even on a weak mobile signal, which matters most to the people this site is for.

The site has since been upgraded to **Astro 7**, without a single visual change. An external marketing partner manages advertising through Google Tag Manager. That setup was left exactly as it was and checked on all 33 pages after every change.

## From a few pages to 33

Instead of a few general pages, every service has **its own page**, written for what people actually search for:

- **Transport of bedridden patients**, **transport of sick people**, **medical transport** and **private ambulance**, all with Belgrade and 24/7 in the heading.
- **Transport to spas** across Serbia.
- **Four pages for patient transport from abroad**: Germany, Austria, Switzerland and Italy, where the diaspora searches for a way to bring a family member home.
- A **price list** for Belgrade, Serbia and Europe, and a contact page that's available around the clock.

## A blog built around keywords and locations

The biggest addition is **a complete blog of 17 articles**, planned around what families search for before they call for transport. It covers four topics:

- **Transport guides**: how to transport a sick patient, transporting bedridden patients, transport abroad and what an ambulance vehicle offers.
- **Spas and destinations**: which conditions are treated in Serbian spas, plus guides to **Vrnjačka Banja, Prolom Banja and Atomska Banja**, with the distance from Belgrade, what is treated and how to get there safely.
- **Health**: hypertension, low blood pressure, atherosclerosis, dementia, osteoporosis, rheumatic diseases and hip fractures in older people. These are the conditions behind most transport needs.
- **Care**: looking after bedridden people and patients confined to bed.

Every article leads to the service it relates to: the articles contain **about 80 links to the service pages**, so readers and Google alike move from a question to the service that answers it. Each article is marked up as a blog post with its date, image and author.

## Every call counted

For a medical transport company **the phone is everything**: nobody fills in a form when they need an ambulance. That's why the phone number is in the header, the hero, the footer and a bar that appears on phones as soon as you scroll. My lead tracking is on every page and **counts each tap on the number**, with the page it came from, so it's clear which services and articles bring the calls.

## Local SEO

- **One main heading per page**, naming the service and the city.
- **Correct canonical addresses**, with no redirects and no links to pages that don't exist.
- **Structured data** describes CARETRANS as a medical business in Belgrade, with its phone, 24/7 hours, the countries it serves and its social profiles. Every service has its own markup, and the FAQ is marked up on the homepage.
- The **FAQ** is plain HTML, so search engines and AI assistants can read it.
- **Titles and descriptions** name Belgrade, the 24/7 service and the phone number, and the sitemap is generated automatically.
