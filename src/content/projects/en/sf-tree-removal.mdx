## The brief

**SF Tree Removal** isn't a marketing site for one business — it's a **lead-generation platform** I designed and built end to end, for a client working the same South Florida tree-service market as [Tree Jaws](/projects/tree-jaws) but solving a different problem: finding and distributing leads at the source, instead of paying to rank for them or bidding on them in Google Ads. That meant a public brand and marketplace on one side, and a real backend with accounts, permissions, and automation on the other.

## Backend, frontend, and the brand itself

I designed the **shield-and-pine logo** and built the whole platform on **Astro with a Supabase (Postgres) backend** — **25+ database tables**, all behind row-level security, covering leads, contractors, truck owners, credits, and audit logs. The public side is the SF Tree Removal marketplace: a permit-leads directory, a contractors page, and a blog. The private side is a password-protected **Admin & Dispatch Command Center** — staff sign in with email/password or Google — showing live totals, a filterable lead queue, and an activity timeline of every automated action the platform takes. There's even a small custom CMS behind it: blog posts are written, edited, and published from inside the dashboard rather than committed as files.

## Compiling permit leads from public records

The core data source is **public permit records**: tree-removal and landscape permits filed with South Florida cities and counties are compiled and scored, then published to a **live, sortable directory** — by date found, permit date, lead score, or jurisdiction — with the filer's name, phone, and email already attached, since that's exactly what's on the public filing. At the time of the screenshots above the directory was running **219 qualified leads** across multiple jurisdictions, all surfaced without a single dollar spent on ads.

## Catching leads other sites miss, and telling people the moment one lands

Permit filings aren't the only signal. The platform also tracks **external leads** — inquiries and signals surfaced from other sites and sources — and routes them through the same pipeline as the directly-sourced ones, so nothing sits in two different systems. The moment a new lead clears that pipeline, the dashboard's automation fires an **availability notification by email to registered contractors and truck owners**, and logs it to the activity timeline so there's a record of exactly who was told and when. Contractors then **claim** a lead and truck owners **apply** for one, both gated behind a credit system, so the whole marketplace runs on accounts and permissions rather than a shared inbox.
