Most owners I talk to have a Facebook page and an Instagram account. They post when they remember, get a few likes from family, and almost never a lead. The conclusion is usually "social doesn't work for our trade."

It does, just not on its own. People don't go to social media to find a plumber the way they go to Google. They go there to scroll. Your job is to be the name they remember before they need you, and to make it effortless to reach you when they do. Here's the order I set it up in for clients, from posting to measuring.

## Decide what counts as a lead

Before you post anything, write down the goal. For most service businesses it's one of these:

- a phone call,
- a text or WhatsApp message,
- a completed quote or booking form.

Likes, followers and reach are not leads. They can help, but they aren't the result. If you can't say at the end of the month how many calls came from social, you can't say whether social works.

## Pick one or two networks, not all of them

One network done well beats four done halfway.

- **Facebook** is still strong for local services, especially with older homeowners and in smaller towns. Local groups are gold if you show up as a neighbor, not an ad.
- **Instagram** fits anything visual: remodels, haircuts, smiles after the dentist, vacation rentals.
- **Nextdoor** (US) is built for neighborhood recommendations, which is exactly how people pick trades.
- **TikTok** brings reach if you have something to show in motion: before and after, how a job is done, quick explainers. It needs consistency.
- **LinkedIn** is for business-to-business work: bookkeeping, commercial cleaning, facility maintenance.

## What to post so people call

People don't call because of a nice logo. They call because they saw you know the job and seem easy to work with. Posts that show that:

1. **Before and after.** A photo or short clip, plus one line on what the problem was and how long it took.
2. **Work in progress.** Ten seconds from a job site says more than any slogan.
3. **Answers to common questions.** "How long does an AC tune-up take?", "Does wisdom tooth removal hurt?". People save and share those.
4. **Customer reviews.** A screenshot of a Google review or a short quote, with permission.
5. **The people behind the business.** Who shows up at the door, who answers the phone.

End every post with how to reach you: a phone number or "send us a message." It sounds obvious, and most posts skip it.

## When to pay for ads

Organic posts build trust, but they reach few people. Ads fix reach. Ads make sense when:

- you have at least ten solid posts, so anyone who taps your profile sees a real business,
- you know your service area and target only that,
- a lead can reach you right away.

Two campaigns are enough to start. The first shows your best video to people in your area. The second shows a call to action to people who watched that video or visited your site. Owners often skip the second one, and it's usually where the cheapest leads come from.

## Lead forms or your website?

Meta offers instant forms people fill in without leaving Facebook or Instagram. They're fast and bring plenty of leads, but a lot of them are "just asking." Sending people to a landing page brings fewer leads, but more serious ones, because the person put in a bit of effort.

My advice: run each for two to three weeks and compare booked jobs, not lead counts.

## Speed to lead decides the job

A social lead is hottest in the first five to ten minutes. After an hour they've messaged two other companies. Make every lead hit your phone as a notification, not an inbox you check at night. If you can't reply right away, an automatic "Got it, we'll call you within the hour" keeps the door open.

## Measure, then decide

For each channel you need five numbers at the end of the month: spend, leads, calls, booked jobs and revenue. With those, the decision is easy: whatever books jobs cheapest gets more budget.

Tagged links (UTM parameters) in posts and ads plus call and WhatsApp click tracking on your site are enough. Without them it all comes down to gut feeling.

## Common mistakes

- Posting only when there's time, so the profile looks abandoned.
- Targeting the whole state when you work in one county.
- Instagram messages left unanswered for days.
- Counting followers instead of calls.
- Every post is an ad ("Sale! Call now!"), so people stop watching.

## The short version

Social media brings leads when you know what a lead is, post things that prove you know the work, pay for ads once your profile looks credible, and reply fast. The rest is measurement.
