Search is changing faster than at any time since Google arrived. Instead of typing a few keywords and scanning ten blue links, people now ask full questions — "who's a reliable tree service near me that handles storm damage?" — and get a written answer from an AI. Google shows AI Overviews above its results. ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Copilot answer millions of questions a day.

That raises a new question for every business: **when AI answers a question about your service, does it mention you?** Generative engine optimisation, or GEO, is the practice of making sure it does.

This article explains what GEO is, how AI search engines decide what to say, how GEO differs from traditional SEO, and what you can start doing today.

## What is generative engine optimisation?

Generative engine optimisation is the process of making your business visible, accurately described and recommended in answers produced by generative AI tools.

Where SEO aims to rank a page in a list of search results, GEO aims to get your business **included in the answer itself** — mentioned by name, cited as a source, or recommended as the best option.

The main "generative engines" today are:

- **Google AI Overviews and AI Mode** — AI summaries inside Google Search
- **ChatGPT** — with built-in web search
- **Perplexity** — an AI answer engine that cites its sources
- **Gemini** — Google's assistant on Android and in Workspace
- **Microsoft Copilot** — inside Bing, Windows and Edge

## How AI search engines decide what to say

Generative engines don't "rank" businesses the way Google's classic algorithm does. In simple terms, most of them work like this:

1. **They interpret the question** and break it into smaller searches.
2. **They retrieve sources** from a search index — web pages, business listings, reviews, videos, forums.
3. **They read and compare those sources**, looking for clear, consistent, trustworthy information.
4. **They write an answer** that summarises what they found, often naming businesses and citing pages.

This tells us what matters. To be included, your information has to be **findable** (in the index), **readable** (easy for a machine to understand), **clear** (stated plainly enough to quote) and **corroborated** (confirmed by other sources).

## GEO vs SEO: what's the difference?

| | SEO | GEO |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Rank pages in search results | Be mentioned, cited or recommended in AI answers |
| Unit of success | A position on a results page | A mention inside an answer |
| User action | Click on a result | Often reads the answer without clicking |
| Main signals | Relevance, links, technical health, experience | Clarity, structured facts, consistency, third-party trust |
| Measurement | Rankings, clicks, conversions | Prompt presence, citations, share of voice, AI-referred leads |

The important part: **GEO doesn't replace SEO, it stands on top of it.** AI tools mostly pull from pages that already rank and that search engines already trust. A site that's invisible in Google is rarely recommended by AI.

## Why GEO matters for your business

### People trust AI recommendations

When an assistant says "here are three highly rated tree services in your area", many people simply call one of the three. Being on that short list is the new page one.

### Zero-click searches are growing

AI answers satisfy many questions without a click. If your SEO strategy only counts clicks, you'll miss the visibility you're gaining — or losing — inside the answers.

### Early movers have an advantage

Most local businesses haven't thought about GEO yet. Clear, well-structured information published now becomes the source AI tools quote for years.

### Wrong information spreads

If your old address, outdated prices or a service you no longer offer are still floating around the web, AI tools may repeat them. GEO includes making sure what AI says about you is correct.

## The building blocks of GEO

### 1. A fast, crawlable website

AI crawlers are less patient than Google. Websites that load content with heavy JavaScript, hide text in sliders or take seconds to load are harder for them to read. Static, lightweight sites — like the Astro websites I build — serve clean HTML that every crawler understands instantly.

### 2. Answer-first content

Structure pages so the answer comes first:

- start each section with a direct, one or two-sentence answer
- then add detail, examples and context
- use clear headings phrased as real questions
- include specific facts: prices, timelines, areas served, licences

A sentence like "Most residential tree removals in South Florida cost between $500 and $2,000, depending on height, access and disposal" is easy for an AI to quote. A paragraph of vague marketing language is not.

### 3. Structured data

Schema markup tells machines exactly what your page is about. For local businesses, the most useful types are:

- `LocalBusiness` (or a specific type like `HomeAndConstructionBusiness`)
- `Service` for each service you offer
- `FAQPage` for question-and-answer content
- `Review` and `AggregateRating`
- `BlogPosting` for articles

### 4. Consistent business information everywhere

Your name, address, phone number, services and service areas should match across your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, industry directories and social profiles. Consistency is how AI systems confirm that the facts are true.

### 5. Reviews and third-party mentions

AI tools put a lot of weight on what others say about you. Reviews, local news coverage, supplier or partner pages, industry associations and community sponsorships all act as independent confirmation that you're real, active and trusted.

### 6. Topical depth

A single "Services" page rarely wins. Businesses that cover their topic thoroughly — a page for each service, guides for common questions, local pages for each area — give AI tools many more reasons and sources to mention them. ## How to get started with GEO this month

1. **Ask the AI tools about yourself.** Search for your business name and your main services in your area in ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google. Note what's said — and what's wrong.
2. **Fix your business profiles.** Update your Google Business Profile and your main directory listings so every detail matches.
3. **Add FAQ sections** to your main service pages, answering real customer questions in plain language.
4. **Add or check your schema markup.** Make sure `LocalBusiness` and `Service` data are present and correct.
5. **Publish one helpful article a month** that answers a question customers ask before they hire you.
6. **Ask happy customers for reviews** that mention the specific service and location.
7. **Start tracking.** Use the metrics from [The AI Search Visibility Metrics and KPIs Every Modern Marketer Should Track](/blog/ai-search-visibility-metrics-kpis).

## Common GEO myths

- **"GEO is a trick to game ChatGPT."** It isn't. It's about publishing clear, true information that machines can read and confirm.
- **"You need a separate GEO website or special file."** You don't. Your normal website, done properly, is your GEO asset. Some sites add an `llms.txt` file to help AI tools find key pages — it's a useful extra, not a requirement.
- **"Only big brands get mentioned."** For local searches, AI tools recommend local businesses. Specific, well-documented small companies often beat big ones.
