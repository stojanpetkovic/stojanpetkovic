A homeowner whose fence blew down last night doesn't always open Google anymore. They might ask their phone: "Who can fix a storm-damaged fence near me this week?" — and an AI assistant answers with two or three company names.

If you're a contractor, that answer is the new page one. This blueprint walks you through four phases to get your business found, understood and recommended by AI search tools: **audit, fix, publish and earn**. No jargon, no tricks — just the work that makes AI tools confident enough to mention you.

## Phase 1: Audit — find out where you stand

You can't fix what you haven't checked. Spend an hour on this before changing anything.

### Ask the AI tools directly

Open ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Copilot and Google (with AI Overviews) and ask questions your customers would ask:

- "Best [your trade] in [your city]"
- "Who does emergency [service] in [your county]?"
- "How much does [service] cost in [your area]?"
- "Is [your business name] a good company?"

Write down:

- Are you mentioned? Where in the answer?
- Which competitors are mentioned instead?
- Which sources are cited?
- Is the information about you correct?

### Find the gaps

Look at the competitors that keep appearing. Usually they have one or more of these: a detailed website with a page for each service, a strong Google Business Profile with many recent reviews, mentions on local or industry sites, or helpful articles that AI tools quote.

Each one is a gap you can close.

## Phase 2: Fix — make your foundations AI-ready

### Fix 1: Speed and crawlability

AI crawlers and search engines need to read your pages quickly and completely. Common problems on contractor websites:

- slow page builders and too many plugins
- text hidden inside images, sliders or pop-ups
- important content loaded only after JavaScript runs
- pages accidentally blocked from indexing

A lightweight website that serves clean HTML solves most of these at once. When [Tree Jaws](/projects/tree-jaws) moved from WordPress to Astro, its pages started loading fast even on job-site mobile connections, and its Lighthouse SEO score reached 100.

### Fix 2: One page per service, one page per area

A single "Services" page listing everything you do gives AI tools very little to work with. Create:

- **a dedicated page for each main service** — what it is, when it's needed, how you do it, typical price ranges, how long it takes, and FAQs
- **service-area pages** for the cities and counties you genuinely serve, each with unique, local content

Quality over quantity: on Tree Jaws, 33 thin, overlapping city pages were consolidated into 20 stronger ones with their own content — and 301-redirected so nothing that already ranked got lost.

### Fix 3: Structured data

Add schema markup so machines can read your key facts without guessing:

- `LocalBusiness` or a specific type like `HomeAndConstructionBusiness` — name, address, phone, hours, service area
- `Service` for each service
- `FAQPage` for question-and-answer sections
- `AggregateRating` and `Review`
- `BlogPosting` for articles

### Fix 4: Consistent business information

Your business name, address, phone number, services, hours and service areas should match exactly on your website, Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, the Better Business Bureau, Angi, Thumbtack, Nextdoor and trade directories. Inconsistent details make AI tools less certain — and uncertain AI tools don't recommend.

### Fix 5: A complete Google Business Profile

Fill in every field: primary and secondary categories, services with descriptions, service areas, hours, photos of real jobs and your team, and regular updates. Answer questions and reply to every review.

## Phase 3: Publish — give AI tools something worth quoting

AI assistants are built to answer questions. Publish clear answers to the questions customers ask before they hire someone.

### What to write about

- **Cost guides:** "How much does tree removal cost in Broward County?"
- **Decision guides:** "Repair or replace: when is a roof past saving?"
- **Process explainers:** "What happens during a free estimate?"
- **Seasonal guides:** "How to prepare your trees for hurricane season"
- **Comparisons:** "Stump grinding vs stump removal"
- **Local regulations:** permits, HOA rules and local requirements

### How to write it so AI can use it

- **Answer first.** Put a direct answer in the first one or two sentences of each section.
- **Be specific.** Use real numbers, timelines, materials and locations.
- **Use question headings.** Phrase H2s and H3s the way people ask.
- **Show experience.** Include photos from your own jobs, real examples and lessons from the field.
- **Keep it current.** Update prices and facts at least once a year.

[Mahalo Tree Service](/projects/mahalo-tree-service) built its organic growth this way: no paid ads, just a faster site and a growing blog of helpful, local articles.

### Don't forget video

AI tools increasingly cite YouTube. Short videos answering common questions — paired with blog posts on the same topic — strengthen both. See [How to Optimize a YouTube Channel: 8 Actionable Steps for Local Leads](/blog/youtube-channel-optimization).

## Phase 4: Earn — build the trust AI tools look for

AI assistants don't just read what you say about yourself; they check what others say.

### Reviews

- Ask every satisfied customer for a Google review — make it easy with a direct link or QR code.
- Encourage customers to mention the service and location in their own words.
- Reply to every review, positive or negative, professionally.
- Collect reviews on other relevant platforms too.

### Third-party mentions

- **Local news:** storm response, community projects, hiring, milestones.
- **Partners and suppliers:** ask to be listed on their "find a contractor" or "partners" pages.
- **Associations:** trade associations and certifications usually include directory listings.
- **Sponsorships:** local sports teams, events and charities often link to sponsors.
- **Community forums and groups:** helpful answers in local groups build recognition.

Each mention is another independent confirmation that you're real, active and trusted in your area.

## A 90-day action plan

**Days 1–30: Audit and fix**
- Run the AI audit and record results
- Fix business information across your main listings
- Complete your Google Business Profile
- Fix website speed and indexing problems
- Add structured data

**Days 31–60: Build pages**
- Create or improve a page for each main service
- Create or improve key service-area pages
- Add FAQ sections to every service page

**Days 61–90: Publish and earn**
- Publish two to four helpful articles
- Start a review request routine
- Secure three to five new third-party mentions
- Repeat the AI audit and compare

## How to measure progress

Repeat your AI audit monthly using the same prompts, and track mentions, citations, accuracy and competitor share. Watch branded searches in Google Search Console and AI referral traffic in Google Analytics, and ask every new customer how they found you. The full measurement framework is in [The AI Search Visibility Metrics and KPIs Every Modern Marketer Should Track](/blog/ai-search-visibility-metrics-kpis).

## Conclusion

Getting found by AI isn't about gaming a new algorithm. It's about making your business easy to read, easy to verify and easy to recommend. Audit where you stand, fix the foundations, publish genuinely useful answers and earn trust beyond your own website. Do that consistently and AI assistants will start saying your name.
